# Proof Atlas: geometric language for formal proofs

Status: active research program. Established 15 October 2026 by the repository owner.

## Goal

Develop a consistent, compositional geometric language that transforms Lean proofs into faithful, navigable visual representations, including massive proofs generated by AI. The language should let a person inspect assumptions, binding, constructions, lemma applications and proof structure at several scales, while retaining access to the original formal object. Visual proof construction with Lean checking is a longer-term objective.

The article reader is an application and evaluation case. Improving an article, drawing a theorem-specific diagram or completing a deployment does not complete this research goal.

## Scientific questions

1. Can a fixed vocabulary of visual primitives represent Lean's kernel expressions and their binding, type and declaration relationships compositionally?
2. Which abstractions reduce visual complexity while preserving explicit boundaries, assumptions and recoverability of the underlying formal terms?
3. Does the same language work for small human-written examples and very large machine-generated proofs, without drawing or expanding every occurrence?
4. Does the representation improve proof inspection, error localization and navigation? These require measured user tasks; graph size and browser checks alone do not answer them.

## Required invariants

- A visible formal object identifies its compiled packet and exact occurrence route. A shared DAG node identifier alone is insufficient for binder meaning.
- Constructor ports retain their names, order and roles. Function/argument, binder domain/body, let type/value/body and projection structure are distinct.
- Bound-variable links follow lexical scope. Binder domains and let values do not acquire the new binder; bodies do. A link outside the current view remains an explicit context boundary.
- Universe levels, declaration references, metadata and unresolved elaborator objects have distinct representations. Unknown context or inferred types are marked unavailable, never invented.
- A collapsed boundary retains its exact root route and recoverable packet reference. Its represented occurrence count is exact; displayed and hidden structure must account for the complete selected region.
- Types, proposition expressions and proof terms remain distinct. A constructor or lemma reference does not by itself establish a logical implication or mathematical explanation.
- Positions, distances and colors are presentation choices unless a specific invariant has been defined and tested. Visual similarity is not mathematical equivalence.
- Every abstraction states whether it preserves syntax, checked typing, definitional equality or a separately reviewed semantic relation. These are different guarantees.

## Research layers

1. **Kernel vocabulary:** the twelve version-pinned Expr constructors, universe levels, ordered ports and scope/reference links.
2. **Structural composition:** occurrence-aware scenes, sharing, exact cuts, navigation and reversible expansion against an immutable packet.
3. **Proof structure:** recognizable introduction/elimination constructions, complete applications, hypotheses and conclusions. Classification must use checked term/type information rather than attractive labels.
4. **Semantic abstraction:** reusable modules and mathematical interpretations with stated assumptions and independently checked correspondence where available.
5. **Source and elaboration:** surface syntax, tactics, commands, macros and elaborator traces, attached through explicit provenance. Kernel expressions do not reconstruct missing tactic histories.
6. **Applications:** article correspondence, proof auditing, generated-proof inspection and eventually visually constructed proofs checked by Lean.

## Evaluation and evidence

Use synthetic adversarial controls, small kernel-checked examples, the published compiled corpus and its separately labelled working snapshot. Include shared terms used under different binders, shadowing, dependent domains, let bindings, projections, universe parameters, open contexts and extreme expansion ratios.

For large proofs record input packet hashes, displayed occurrence budget, frontier accounting, scope-link checks and bounded-memory traversal. Current large packets are scale tests; do not label them an AI-generated benchmark without recorded generation provenance. Add diverse independently sourced and AI-generated proofs when their provenance and publication rights are established.

Public packet checks establish serialized structural correspondence. Independent kernel replay, visual-language adequacy, semantic compression and human comprehension require their own evidence. The existing constructor catalogue and article-specific diagrams are useful foundations, not a completed general language.

## Working rule

Prioritize general language rules and falsifiable tests over article-specific styling. Each iteration should identify a representation problem, implement or reject a rule, and retain its evidence and limitations. Keep the program active across application releases. Publish only significant supported positive or negative scientific results in the blog; keep routine software checks and operational records in technical evidence.

First implementation milestone: an article-independent, budgeted geometric scene representation with ordered ports, exact occurrence paths, lexical-scope links and complete frontier accounting, tested on small adversarial examples and both compiled inventories. This milestone is part of the program, not its completion.


## First structural implementation

The optional [scoped geometry prototype](/projects/proof-atlas/library/?proof=JacobiCore.E_pos&geometry=scoped) is independent of the article map. Its versioned scene contract retains all twelve kernel constructor kinds, ordered child ports, occurrence-qualified vertices, lexical-scope links and exact collapsed boundaries. Universe and other constructor payloads remain attached; they are not yet a complete geometric notation of their own.

A scene is a finite cut of the expanded occurrence tree, constructed directly from the shared packet. Each displayed occurrence contributes one; a frontier contributes the number of descendants hidden below it. Their sum must equal the selected root's exact expanded count. External binder targets remain explicit context boundaries. `domain` is the exporter's display alias for Lean's `binderType`; the scene records both port names. The cut is recoverable against its packet, not a standalone proof certificate.

The audit currently checks 6,166 cuts across all 2,098 packets and 4,972 lexical-scope links. Three synthetic scope controls include a shared bound-variable node with different binders, a let value outside its new binder and an enclosing binder outside the current cut. These controls are structural tests, not additional kernel certificates.

In the real `JacobiCore.E_pos` proof, shared node 17 appears on two displayed routes and points to different binders. The prototype draws separate occurrences. The initial largest proof-body cut shows 9 occurrences and records 462,476,262 hidden descendants, accounting for 462,476,271 body occurrences. This verifies the cut's accounting, not meaningful semantic compression or human comprehension. The depth cut can stop in a binder telescope before exposing the mathematical argument; structured telescope abstraction is a subsequent research problem.

Reproduce with `node scripts/test-proof-atlas-geometry-core.cjs`; after the production site build, run `node scripts/test-proof-atlas-geometry-browser.cjs`. The browser checks real canvas/keyboard selections, distinct scope targets, exact boundary refinement, saved routes and the large body at phone/desktop widths in both themes.
