What the ordering means

A solid box is an input; a dashed box is a local computation. The two marked boxes are the adjacent pair being exchanged. Vertical order is lexical scope. It is not evaluation time or a proof-dependency edge. Binder names may repeat: an address includes its state and field path.

Each exchange decreases the number of let-before-input pairs by one. Inputs retain their relative order, as do computations. The body and domain changes are capture avoiding. The inverse consumes the recorded steps in reverse order, checking each intermediate owner. Removing or reordering a residual does not establish source recovery.

The view retains one small control, with at most 4,096 source rows, 4,096 native rows, 4,096 steps and 128 binder rows per column. Constructor inspection retains one selected node and at most three children. This does not establish a bound for all browser memory or a paged normalization of arbitrary proofs.

Corpus boundary

All 18 recorded output closures of the selected Riccati and AI-source proofs were checked structurally. Six Riccati closures require two exchanges each; their two schedules agree literally and reverse exactly. New typing of those corpus terms remains unavailable. The original two proof-body spines and the eight AI-source closures have no exchanges. They are not substitutes for wider corpus evaluation.

From local definitions to boundary inputs

A retained computation stays inside the function. A selected external definition becomes an independently supplied input, at its original type. Normalization then transports the input domains past retained computations. Supplying the original values recovers the original declared goal; arbitrary new values can change the result family.

Ten authored interfaces pass fresh kernel checks. Two other original programs pass, but their promoted interfaces are rejected: one relies on a fixed finite index, the other on a type alias being concretely Nat. Rejected candidates have no inferred interface typing.
